# CBT-Style Thought Reflection

*Educational wellness resource - a self-reflection exercise, not psychotherapy.*

## The idea
Cognitive behavioural approaches suggest that the way we interpret a situation influences how we feel. Self-reflection exercises can help you slow down and look at a thought from more than one angle. Thoughts are not always facts.

## Steps of a thought record
- **Situation.** What happened? Where were you, and who was involved? Stick to facts.
- **Automatic thought.** What went through your mind? For example, "I always mess things up."
- **Evidence for.** What facts support this thought?
- **Evidence against.** What facts do not fit this thought? What would you say to a friend in the same situation?
- **Balanced thought.** Write a fairer sentence that takes both sides into account, such as "I made a mistake this time, but I have also handled many things well."
- **Reflection.** Notice how you feel now, and whether anything shifted, even slightly.

## Common unhelpful thinking patterns
All-or-nothing thinking ("it was a total failure"). Mind reading ("they must think I'm boring"). Catastrophising ("this will ruin everything"). Overgeneralising ("this always happens"). Noticing a pattern can make it easier to question.

## Helpful questions
Is there another way to look at this? What would I tell a friend? What is within my control right now? What is one small step I could take?
